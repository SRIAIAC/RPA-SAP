# Maintenance — Rules & SOPs

1. **Work order priority classification.** Emergency (safety/production down):
   response within 1 hour. Urgent: within 8 hours. Routine: scheduled within 5
   business days. Priority is set by the alert source (SCADA/IoT severity code)
   and can only be downgraded by a Maintenance Senior Manager, never upgraded
   away.
2. **Equipment identification requirement.** A work order cannot be created
   without a valid SAP Asset Master equipment ID; alerts referencing unknown
   equipment IDs are held in a data-quality queue pending asset-master
   correction, not silently dropped.
3. **Duplicate work order prevention.** Before creating a new notification, an
   open notification/work order against the same equipment within the last 72
   hours is checked; if found, the new alert is appended as a note rather than
   creating a duplicate work order.
4. **Technician skill-group matching.** Work orders are assigned only to
   technicians certified in the required skill group (electrical, rotating
   equipment, instrumentation, etc.); if none are available in the current
   shift, the work order is escalated to the shift supervisor for
   cross-shift/contractor coverage.
5. **Spare parts reservation.** A work order requiring spares checks Material
   Management stock before scheduling; a shortage blocks scheduling and
   auto-triggers the Spare Parts Replenishment workflow rather than starting
   work without parts on hand.
6. **Preventive maintenance (PM) compliance.** PM work orders generated by the
   SAP PM maintenance plan must be completed within their due window (typically
   +/- 5 days); overdue PMs beyond 10 days are escalated and reported in the
   monthly compliance dashboard.
7. **Permit-to-work dependency.** Maintenance work requiring hot work,
   confined space, or LOTO cannot be scheduled or started without the
   corresponding HSE-issued permit referenced on the work order (see HSE KB
   rule 4).
8. **Shift handover completeness.** Every shift handover report must include:
   open work orders with status, active alarms, any LOTO in effect, and
   production-impacting issues; an incomplete handover (missing any section)
   is rejected back to the outgoing shift lead.
9. **Breakdown vs. planned maintenance coding.** Every completed work order is
   coded breakdown or planned for reliability (MTBF) reporting; miscoding is
   caught by a monthly reconciliation against the alert source (SCADA-
   triggered = breakdown by default).
10. **Critical asset redundancy check.** Before taking a critical asset (rated
    "single point of failure" in the asset master) offline for maintenance,
    the workflow confirms redundant capacity is available or explicitly logs
    a production-risk acceptance from the Production Department Head.
11. **Work order closure requirements.** A work order cannot be closed without
    technician notes, actual hours logged, and parts consumed recorded against
    the equipment history — required for warranty claims and reliability
    analysis.
12. **Contractor maintenance work.** External contractor work orders require
    the contractor's insurance and permit-to-work documentation validated
    (per Procurement KB rule 4 and HSE KB rule 4) before work is authorized to
    start.
